# Cancer Terms Explained — guide for your November 8 demo

## Goal

Support family caregivers who don't understand unfamiliar words used in cancer appointments and bills, without pretending to diagnose or interpret a personal medical record.

## What is built

- 61 words and acronyms across six categories: Diagnosis, Treatment, Tests and scans, Side effects, Care and support, Money and insurance.
- Search as you type, categories, quick-lookup buttons, and alternate names (such as “chemo”).
- Each result includes a plain-language description, why someone might hear it, a suggested question for the care team, and an official source.
- A caregiver typing “chemotherapy” in the intake sees a “Words you mentioned” shortcut under the personalized next steps.
- Optional navigation from the welcome page or sidebar.

## 90-second demo

1. Open **Cancer terms explained** from the sidebar.
2. Click **CBC**. Explain the abbreviation and open “Why you might hear it.”
3. Search for **metastasis** or **neutropenia** and point to an authoritative reference link.
4. Click the **palliative care** quick lookup, then **hospice care** to show the difference.
5. Return to **Find support**, use the sample scenario and generate recommendations. Click a term in **Words you mentioned** to navigate to the glossary.

## Sources / limitations

Material was written as short educational summaries with authoritative source links from:

- NCI cancer terms: https://www.cancer.gov/publications/dictionaries/cancer-terms
- ACS glossary: https://www.cancer.org/cancer/understanding-cancer/glossary.html
- HealthCare.gov glossary: https://www.healthcare.gov/glossary/
- NCI on palliative care: https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/advanced-cancer/care-choices/palliative-care-fact-sheet
- NCI on infection: https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/treatment/side-effects/infection

The summaries are not individualized clinical advice and have not undergone clinician review. Before a public pilot with patients, ask the faculty mentor and oncology content reviewer to validate every definition, context phrase, and link.
